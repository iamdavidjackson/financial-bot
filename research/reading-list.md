### [East India Company - Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/East-India-Company)

**Why read it:** Useful for understanding the joint-stock company model and the early history of tradable company ownership.

**Topics covered:**
- Joint-stock ownership
- Early shareholder structures
- Links between trade, empire, and financial markets

**Notes:**

- Created by Royal Proclamation in 1600
- acted as agent of British imperialism in Asia through mid 1800s
- employed it's own army
- numerous wars through the 17th and 18th centuries expanded influence
- extracted resources through taxation and slave labor
- started trading tea with China in early 1800s
- ceased to exist in 1873 when the Crown assumed control and installed Raj

### [South Sea Company - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sea_Company)

**Why read it:** Useful for understanding early stock speculation, financial bubbles, and the links between state debt and equity markets.

**Topics covered:**
- Early speculative bubbles
- State finance and markets
- Investor behavior in emerging capital markets

**Notes:**

- Founded in 1711 by Robert Harley as a way to consolidate government debt of 9 million pounds.  The government would pay investors 568,279 pounds a year with a 6% interest
- in 1713 granted a monopoly to supply enslaved Africans to the "South Seas" and South America.
- Never benefitted from trade as there were active wars however the company stock rose greatly 
- Insider trading made many huge amounts of money by buying debt ahead of consolidation however many public investors lost vast amounts
- Company crashes in 1720, often called "South Sea Bubble" and reduced public trust in companies
- Isaac Newton suffered massive losses


### [Bubble Act - Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubble_Act)

**Why read it:** Useful for understanding how governments responded to speculative bubbles and how regulation affected the development of joint-stock companies.

**Topics covered:**
- Regulatory response to speculation
- Early company restrictions
- Market trust and financial law

**Notes:**

- Law passed in 1720 in response to South Sea Bubble
- Also known as Royal Exchange and London Assurance Corporation Act 1719
- Forbade the creation of companies without royal assent
- Created the London Assurance Company which dealt in marine insurance
